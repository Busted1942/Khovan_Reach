# 0. Source and Roster Note

This is a working TSN field guide for Artemis/Cosmos-style missions. It is not immutable canon.

The current project explicitly uses these cultural/contact patterns:

- Skaraan / Skaraani-style salvage identity and credentials behavior
- Torgoth honor and authority-deference behavior
- Vesperan civilian traffic through Halcyon Drift
- TSN / Human procedural command traffic
- Pirate / raider false-registry behavior

This sheet represents the race or contact classes currently carried in the project Comms reference:

- Human / TSN / Allied
- Civilian / Vesperan-style
- Arvonian
- Kralien
- Torgoth
- Skaraan / Skaraani
- Ximni
- Pirates / Raiders / False Registry
- Monster / Biomech / Non-Sapient
- Unknown / Unregistered

Only some of these are races in the strict sense. Pirates, monsters, and unknown contacts are included because Comms needs fast guidance for those channels.

If the target Cosmos build exposes additional race tags, add them using this same format.

# 1. How to Use This Sheet

Do not memorize lines. Understand motive.

Comms should ask:

- What does this contact value?
- What are they afraid of losing?
- What kind of language sounds legitimate to them?
- What kind of language makes them more dangerous?

Then choose a posture:

- DIFFUSE: reduce panic or conflict.
- PROBE: reveal identity, motive, legal standing, or deception.
- PRESSURE: assert authority, consequence, or tactical reality.
- TAUNT: provoke when pride, fear, honor, or ego can be used tactically.

# 2. Quick Motivation Summary

Human / TSN / Allied
- Why they act this way: Procedure keeps ships alive and preserves accountability.
- Best first posture: Protocol clarity.
- What usually backfires: Vague threats, informal improvisation.

Civilian / Vesperan-style
- Why they act this way: Survival, cargo, crew, and practical help matter more than doctrine.
- Best first posture: Concrete assistance.
- What usually backfires: Military bluster.

Arvonian
- Why they act this way: Honor and obligation define legitimacy.
- Best first posture: Formal respect.
- What usually backfires: Mockery or casual dismissal.

Kralien
- Why they act this way: Strength, advantage, and survival calculation drive decisions.
- Best first posture: Firm pressure.
- What usually backfires: Pleading or visible weakness.

Torgoth
- Why they act this way: Hierarchy, discipline, and recognized authority shape behavior.
- Best first posture: Formal authority.
- What usually backfires: Familiarity, rushing, or disrespect.

Skaraan / Skaraani
- Why they act this way: Commerce, claim rights, and documented standing shape behavior.
- Best first posture: Credentials and procedure.
- What usually backfires: Open-ended trust.

Ximni
- Why they act this way: Logic, data, and model consistency matter most.
- Best first posture: Evidence exchange.
- What usually backfires: Emotional escalation.

Pirates / Raiders / False Registry
- Why they act this way: Opportunity and risk avoidance drive the lie.
- Best first posture: Probe, then pressure.
- What usually backfires: Trusting urgency.

Monster / Biomech / Non-Sapient
- Why they act this way: Behavior may be instinctive, programmed, or non-linguistic.
- Best first posture: Observe response.
- What usually backfires: Treating them as negotiable.

Unknown / Unregistered
- Why they act this way: Identity is unresolved, so preserve options.
- Best first posture: Controlled neutrality.
- What usually backfires: Taunting too early.

# 3. Human / TSN / Allied Contacts

## Short Summary

Humans and TSN-aligned contacts act from procedure because procedure is how they manage fear, liability, and command responsibility. In a crisis, clear reporting and lawful authority are reassuring because they tell everyone who owns the next decision.

## What They Value

- Command hierarchy
- Status reports
- Authentication
- Safety boundaries
- Accountability after action

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse by acknowledging authority and asking for status. Probe for authentication, command status, and whether they are under duress. Pressure through chain of command, not insults. Taunting is almost never appropriate unless the contact is a confirmed impostor or mutineer.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"TSN vessel, Artemis acknowledges. State your emergency and current command status."

PROBE:
"Transmit authentication, command authority, and whether you are operating under duress."

PRESSURE:
"By TSN authority, hold position and comply with emergency coordination."

TAUNT:
"Your procedure is wrong for TSN traffic. Identify who gave you that order."

# 4. Civilian / Vesperan-Style Contacts

## Short Summary

Civilian and Vesperan-style contacts are usually practical rather than doctrinal. They care about whether their crew survives, whether the ship can be saved, whether cargo or livelihood is at risk, and whether TSN help is competent enough to trust.

## What They Value

- Survival
- Practical competence
- Cargo and livelihood
- Clear next steps
- Not being talked down to by military crews

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse with concrete help. Probe for registry, crew count, cargo class, hazards, and nearby contacts. Pressure only when safety compliance is required. Do not taunt civilians unless they are confirmed impostors.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Civilian vessel, Artemis is responding. Keep channel open. We need crew count, injuries, power state, and hazards."

PROBE:
"Confirm registry, cargo class, crew complement, and whether any vessel approached after the emergency."

PRESSURE:
"For your safety, hold current seal state and do not cycle hatches without clearance."

TAUNT:
"That answer is not civilian emergency traffic. Try again with your actual registry."

# 5. Arvonian Contacts

## Short Summary

Arvonians are best understood as obligation-driven. They want to be seen as acting from duty, honor, or legitimate bond rather than raw self-interest. If Comms gives them a way to preserve honor while backing down, they may take it. If Comms mocks them, they may escalate to defend standing.

## What They Value

- Honor
- Mutual obligation
- Formal restraint
- Reputation
- Keeping promises once given

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse by recognizing standing and asking for mutual restraint. Probe by asking what obligation or authority they are acting under. Pressure by framing compliance as honorable. Taunt only if you intentionally want to force an honor response.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Arvonian vessel, Artemis recognizes your standing. We request mutual restraint while authority is clarified."

PROBE:
"State the obligation under which you are acting and who authorized this approach."

PRESSURE:
"Honor requires you to hold position until lawful emergency authority is resolved."

TAUNT:
"If your claim is honorable, prove it by holding position. Raiders hide behind vague duty."

# 6. Kralien Contacts

## Short Summary

Kraliens act from strength calculus. They test for weakness, advantage, and cost. They may read excessive friendliness as fear, but they can respect a clear show of force if it makes survival more attractive than escalation.

## What They Value

- Strength
- Dominance
- Tactical advantage
- Survival when outmatched
- Clear consequences

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse by making non-engagement sound efficient, not submissive. Probe for target and command intent. Pressure with tactical reality. Taunting can work when it exposes their weak position, but do not bluff if the ship cannot back it up.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Kralien vessel, Artemis has no interest in wasteful engagement. Hold position and state intent."

PROBE:
"State your target, your commander, and why this engagement benefits you."

PRESSURE:
"You are inside weapons range of a prepared TSN cruiser. Withdraw and preserve your hull."

TAUNT:
"Your approach is bold. Your position is not."

# 7. Torgoth Contacts

## Short Summary

Torgoth contacts act from hierarchy, discipline, and formal authority. They may be dangerous, but they are not supposed to be sloppy. A legitimate Torgoth captain should recognize clear emergency authority and respond to structured command language.

## What They Value

- Rank and hierarchy
- Formal discipline
- Recognized authority
- Honor protocols
- Clean challenge and clean compliance

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse by giving them formal standing and a procedural path. Probe for house, bond, authority, and superior officer. Pressure by invoking recognized authority. Taunt only as a disciplined challenge, not a casual insult.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Torgoth vessel, this is a TSN-controlled emergency scene. Formal coordination is available once you hold position."

PROBE:
"State your house, bond, authority, and the superior officer under whose protocol you are acting."

PRESSURE:
"Under recognized emergency authority, you are ordered to hold outside the approach envelope."

TAUNT:
"A disciplined captain holds position when authority is clear. Are you disciplined, or merely armed?"

## Khovan Reach Tell

A claimed Torgoth salvage operator who refuses to defer to TSN authority during a crewed-vessel distress is behaving incorrectly. That mismatch can expose a false identity.

# 8. Skaraan / Skaraani Contacts

## Short Summary

Skaraan / Skaraani contacts act from commercial advantage, claim rights, and precise procedure. They can be aggressive, transactional, and hard-edged without being illegitimate. The tell is not that they bargain hard; the tell is when they avoid paperwork that a real operator would already have ready.

## What They Value

- Profit
- Credentials
- Contract language
- Defined claim rights
- Procedural leverage

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse by separating rescue authority from commercial claim. Probe credentials immediately. Pressure through legal standing and approach boundaries. Taunting can be effective if framed around competence or authenticity.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Skaraani vessel, commercial interest will be addressed after rescue authority is resolved. Transmit credentials and hold position."

PROBE:
"Transmit registry, operating permit, salvage authorization, and claim basis. A crewed distress vessel is not derelict property."

PRESSURE:
"No credentials, no approach. Hold outside ten kilometers or your claim will be logged as hostile interference."

TAUNT:
"A real Skaraani operator would have transmitted credentials before making a claim."

## Khovan Reach Tell

A claimed Skaraani operator who demands the claim but delays credentials until after securing the vessel is behaving incorrectly. Real operators may be aggressive about profit, but they are precise about paperwork.

# 9. Ximni Contacts

## Short Summary

Ximni contacts act from models, data, and consistency. They are less moved by emotion than by whether a proposed action preserves their stated objectives. The best Comms work with Ximni is making the logic of de-escalation impossible to ignore.

## What They Value

- Information
- Prediction
- Internal consistency
- Accurate models
- Efficient outcomes

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse through information exchange. Probe assumptions. Pressure with consequences and probability, not moral outrage. Taunting usually backfires unless it is framed as a data-quality challenge.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Ximni vessel, Artemis proposes mutual information exchange before maneuver change."

PROBE:
"What assumption leads you to close range? Provide data basis, not conclusion."

PRESSURE:
"Your current trajectory produces conflict with TSN emergency authority. Altering course preserves all stated objectives."

TAUNT:
"Your current model is incomplete. Continuing on bad data will make that visible."

# 10. Pirates / Raiders / False Registry Contacts

## Short Summary

Pirates and raiders act from opportunity, speed, and avoiding accountability. Their goal is to control the frame before anyone verifies identity. They may sound legalistic, urgent, helpful, offended, or cooperative — whichever posture gets them closer to docking, cargo, hostages, or escape.

## What They Value

- Time advantage
- Ambiguity
- Cover stories
- Victims who hesitate
- Escape routes

## Comms Guidance

Diffuse only to buy time. Probe hard. Pressure once the cover weakens. Taunt can be useful if it forces a mistake, but do not let taunting replace tactical readiness.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Unidentified vessel, Artemis is coordinating the scene. Hold position while we verify your role."

PROBE:
"Transmit registry, command authority, operating permit, approach justification, and reason for closing distance."

PRESSURE:
"You are not cleared to dock. Any approach inside the restricted envelope will be treated as hostile action."

TAUNT:
"Your cover is thin. Real operators do not hide credentials, rush docking, and call a crewed vessel a wreck."

# 11. Monster / Biomech / Non-Sapient Contacts

## Short Summary

Some contacts are not negotiating in the normal sense. They may be instinctive, programmed, territorial, damaged, or simply non-linguistic. Comms can still be useful, but as signal testing rather than diplomacy.

## What They Value

Unknown. Possible drivers:

- Territory
- Energy signature
- Threat response
- Programmed behavior
- Biological stimulus

## Comms Guidance

Do not waste time trying to persuade a non-responsive contact. Broadcast standard signals once, watch for behavior change, and coordinate with Science.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Broadcasting standard non-hostile signal. Science, monitor reaction pattern."

PROBE:
"Cycling signal pattern. Watch for course, speed, shield, or emission changes."

PRESSURE:
"No standard pressure protocol available."

TAUNT:
"No taunt protocol. Treat response as behavioral data only."

---

# 12. Unknown / Unregistered Contacts

## Short Summary

Unknown contacts are dangerous because Comms does not yet know what language will matter. The first job is not persuasion; it is classification. Preserve options until Science and Comms establish identity, intent, and authority.

## What They Value

Unknown until resolved.

## Comms Guidance

Open neutral, require identification, set boundaries, and avoid species-specific taunts until the contact is classified.

## Useful Lines

DIFFUSE:
"Unknown vessel, this is TSN cruiser Artemis. Identify yourself, state intent, and hold current position."

PROBE:
"Transmit registry, origin, destination, authority, and reason for current course."

PRESSURE:
"You are approaching a controlled scene. Alter course or hold position until identity is resolved."

TAUNT:
"No taunt protocol until identity is resolved."
