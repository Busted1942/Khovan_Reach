# KHOVAN REACH — STRUCTURAL OUTLINE

*Working title for the standalone TSN training scenario. Updated design spec incorporating the investigation-first mission framing and the pirate-under-salvager-cover Act III threat. This is the outline source — not the final scenario document.*

---

## REVISION NOTE

This revision replaces the original fetch-mission premise with an investigation premise: Artemis is diverted from a qualification cruise to investigate a fragmentary TSN-compatible distress signal in the Khovan Reach region. The cache retrieval still occurs, but it is discovered in the field after Engineering diagnoses *Halcyon Drift* and identifies the needed component.

This revision also bakes in the Act I teach-then-transfer drill update: Drill Two is a guided contact-handling sequence ending in Weapons subsystem disable, and Drill Three is the unprompted repeat against a simply evasive drone ending in Engine subsystem disable.

This revision also replaces legitimate independent salvagers with opportunistic pirates initially presenting as salvage operators. Their motive is clean and local: they picked up the distress signal, saw a damaged civilian ship, and intend to exploit it. They may be exposed through clever Comms hailing, legal/registry probing, cultural mismatch, Science scans, or through escalation after they are denied docking with *Halcyon Drift*.

---

## 1. MISSION CONCEPT

Khovan Reach is a complete TSN mission scenario with an embedded qualification framework. The fiction is foreground; qualification cards are GM-facing observations made during play and revealed only at debrief.

A new TSN crew on the cruiser Artemis begins with a routine shakedown and qualification cruise. During the cruise, Command diverts them to investigate a fragmentary distress signal in the Khovan Reach region. The signal is on a TSN-compatible frequency band, but it is too degraded for Command to identify the source, condition, or authenticity with full confidence. Artemis is the closest cruiser-class asset with operational capability.

The crew finds a damaged civilian cargo hauler, *Halcyon Drift*, whose people are in genuine distress. During Engineering's away-mission diagnosis, the crew discovers that the repair requires a quantum field stabilizer unit stocked at a TSN scientific cache near Khovan Reach. The mission converges in the field: investigate the signal, diagnose the casualty, retrieve the component, save the people.

Act III adds an external threat. Two vessels arrive claiming to be independent salvagers. They are not legitimate salvagers; they are opportunistic pirates using salvage cover to get close to a damaged civilian ship. Their pretense can be exposed by sharp Comms work, corroborated by Science scans, or revealed through escalation when they are denied docking with *Halcyon Drift*.

The mission tests six stations through a real scenario rather than through isolated drill checks. Helm has to dock, transit, station-keep, and maneuver under combat conditions. Weapons has to demonstrate targeting in drills, then manage real engagement timing and torpedo reserves. Engineering has to balance power, deploy as an away team, diagnose *Halcyon Drift*, and face a personal-stakes mitigation decision. Science has to identify contacts, read long-range signals, assess damage, classify pirate signatures, and select the correct cache component. Comms has to handle station hails, Command cadence, away-team relay, DAMCON reports, and deception detection under pressure. Captain has to investigate under uncertainty, allocate resources, decide whether Engineering stays exposed, decide whether to convert torpedoes for speed, and authorize force at the right time.

The mission is intentionally lighter in moral temperature than Sigma Protocol. There is no hidden conspiracy, no doctrine confession, no campaign seed required. The dramatic weight comes from competence under pressure, command timing, and real costs. The DAMCON team may die. *Halcyon Drift* may be lost. Engineering may be physically exposed. Pirates may force a combat decision. Failures matter, but consequences are recoverable rather than campaign-ending.

## 2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

**Console play is the default.** Everyone stays at their station for the mission except during the brief Engineering away mission to *Halcyon Drift*. The scenario is played through the SBS consoles, not through extended GM narration.

**Voice-mode AI is limited and deliberate.** One GPT-4o NPC appears: Hessler aboard *Halcyon Drift*, in conversation with the Engineering player during the away mission. The conversation is observed by the whole table. No other voice-mode characters are required. Pirates, DAMCON team, Anderson, and Dillon communicate through recorded clips, scripted comms exchanges, or GM voice.

**Qualification is observation, not testing.** Cards are GM-only. Players never see them during play. The mission is played as a real mission. Dillon's debrief surfaces the cards and walks through them with the crew. This produces better play because players engage with the fiction, not the rubric.

**No-fail with real costs.** Catastrophic failures, such as ship destruction, trigger a MAST state-save reload to the last drill checkpoint with prior qualifications preserved. Non-catastrophic failures, such as DAMCON casualties, pirate damage, depleted weapons, or *Halcyon Drift* loss, are absorbed into qualification cards and acknowledged at debrief. The team always finishes the session, but not every outcome is clean.

**Reactive, not procedural.** Anderson does not dispatch Artemis to fetch a known part. Anderson dispatches Artemis to investigate an uncertain distress signal. The cache run develops from field diagnosis after Engineering identifies what *Halcyon Drift* needs and where the nearest compatible component is. This avoids the contrivance of Command sending the crew for the exact part they will happen to need later.

**Weapons must have a real live-fire role.** The earlier salvager-only version over-served Comms and left Weapons under-exercised. The pirate-cover revision gives Comms the first qualification beat — detect the deception or mishandle the hailing sequence — and then gives Weapons a real Act III combat beat if the situation goes hot.

**Standalone, not a campaign starter.** Khovan Reach is one mission. It does not lead into Sigma Protocol or any other scenario. Dillon may continue as a familiar instructor voice, but no narrative continuity is required.

## 3. THE FICTION

### Setting

TSN cruiser Artemis has just left Kestrel Yards after a refit cycle. New crew complement. Energy reserves are at 70% of full standard load; kinetic weapons are at 0: no homing torpedoes, no nukes, no EMPs, no mines. The first task is a shakedown qualification cruise intended to verify that the crew can operate together before assignment to routine patrol duty.

Dillon is the embedded instructor. He has authority over the qualification process and consultative input to the captain, but he is not in the operational chain of command. The captain commands the ship; Dillon observes and advises sparingly.

### The qualification cruise

The cruise begins as a standard three-part verification sequence:

1. Depart Kestrel Yards and dock at Tarsis Station for resupply.
2. Conduct a guided contact-handling drill with a marked passive drone, ending in controlled Weapons subsystem disable.
3. Conduct an unguided evasive live-fire repeat with a second marked drone, ending in Engine subsystem disable.

The purpose is to establish station competence and crew coordination before the scenario pivots to live operations.

### Anderson's diversion order

Approximately one hour into the qualification cruise, Command transmits new orders. A fragmentary distress signal has been detected in the Khovan Reach region by long-range monitoring. The signal uses a TSN-compatible emergency band, but it is too degraded for definitive identification. The pattern could indicate a civilian emergency beacon, a TSN vessel in distress, a relay artifact, or a deliberate lure. Command cannot resolve it from range.

Artemis is the closest cruiser-class ship with operational capability. Orders: proceed toward the Khovan Reach region, investigate the signal source, render aid as appropriate, and report findings. Dillon notes that the qualification framework remains in effect because this is now a live operation under observation.

The captain is not told that a quantum field stabilizer is needed. The captain is not told that a cache run will be required. The cache is known only as regional context: TSN maintains forward-deployed scientific caches in and around Khovan Reach, as it does in several lightly patrolled regions.

### Signal localization

As Artemis enters the outer Khovan Reach envelope, Science begins to resolve the signal. It is authentic enough to warrant action: degraded, intermittent, but consistent with a real emergency beacon rather than a clean tactical lure. Science triangulates the source to a single vessel approximately fifteen minutes off Artemis's direct approach vector.

This is not a side quest away from a fetch mission. This is the investigation Anderson ordered. The captain's decision is whether to act decisively on imperfect but sufficient information. The intended action is to deviate to the signal source and render aid.

### Halcyon Drift's situation

*Halcyon Drift* is a mid-sized Vesperan-registered civilian cargo hauler operating a legal route through the region. Its primary reactor experienced a cascading regulator failure approximately two hours before Artemis's arrival. The crew successfully shut down the reactor before catastrophic failure, but the ship is running on emergency power. Life support is degraded, hull integrity is compromised in two sections, and several crew are injured.

Captain Hessler is exhausted but in command. Crew count: seven. Three are injured. Hessler requests professional engineering assistance because his ship cannot safely restart or stabilize the reactor without outside help.

The damage is consistent with a manufacturing defect in the regulator assembly — a known issue with this class of regulator that TSN technical bulletins have flagged, but Vesperan civilian operators have been slow to retrofit. The repair requires a compatible quantum field stabilizer unit.

### The convergence

Engineering discovers the convergence during the away mission. Through diagnostic work and Hessler's report, Engineering identifies that *Halcyon Drift* needs a quantum field stabilizer rated for civilian power systems. Hessler knows the ship needs this class of part but does not have one aboard.

Engineering or Science then realizes that the nearest plausible source is the TSN scientific cache near Khovan Reach. The cache routinely stocks stabilizer units for forward-deployed scientific and support missions. The rescue is now dependent on a cache retrieval.

This matters structurally. Command did not know the needed part in advance. The crew discovers the problem and solution under pressure. The cache run becomes an emergency response, not a pre-scripted errand.

### The cascade

During initial repair preparation, a secondary structural element gives way. Atmospheric loss begins in the engineering section where the DAMCON team is working. Blast doors seal automatically as a safety response. Three DAMCON team members are isolated in a section with active atmospheric loss and limited suit O2 reserves.

The cascade is not caused by player action. It is a consequence of *Halcyon Drift* being already damaged. Hessler is apologetic and concerned. The Engineering player is aboard *Halcyon Drift* when the cascade occurs.

Engineering can slow atmospheric loss through hands-on mitigation if they remain aboard. If Engineering returns to Artemis, the atmospheric loss accelerates and the DAMCON timer compresses.

### The pirate arrival

Approximately twenty minutes after the cascade, two unknown vessels arrive near *Halcyon Drift*. They identify as independent salvage operators. Their stated claim is that they picked up the distress beacon, arrived to investigate, and have authority to secure the damaged vessel.

They are pirates.

Their motive is clean and local. They are not connected to a larger conspiracy, syndicate arc, or campaign thread. They picked up the distress signal, recognized an isolated civilian ship in distress, and saw an opportunity to board, kill witnesses, and strip cargo. Their salvage identity is a cover to delay TSN action and get close enough to dock.

The law is clear: a crewed vessel broadcasting distress is not salvage. It must be rendered aid. Legitimate salvage rights do not apply while crew are alive and requesting assistance. The pirate cover can therefore crack under Comms scrutiny.

### Pirate reveal paths

There are two intended reveal paths.

**Path 1: clever hailing exposes them early.** Comms asks for salvage credentials, registry confirmation, operating permits, emergency-assistance authority, or acknowledgment that *Halcyon Drift* is a crewed vessel in distress. The pirates evade, misuse legal language, call the ship a wreck too early, refuse to transmit proper credentials, or violate expected cultural protocols. Science can reinforce the read by detecting weapons signatures inconsistent with civilian salvage equipment.

**Path 2: denial of docking exposes them by escalation.** If Comms does not expose them through hailing, the pirates request docking with *Halcyon Drift*. Captain, Hessler, or Engineering denies docking authorization. The pirates hold briefly, then attempt unauthorized docking or move into attack posture. At that point the cover collapses and the scene becomes a combat problem.

This design gives Comms a meaningful deception-detection check without making the entire Act III conflict a negotiation. It also gives Weapons a real live-fire qualification beat if the situation goes hot.

### Time budget

From cascade to total atmospheric loss in the trapped section: approximately 30 minutes of real game time, compressed based on whether Engineering remains on *Halcyon Drift*.

- Engineer aboard *Halcyon Drift*: timer expires at T+30.
- Engineer aboard Artemis: timer effectively compresses to T+15.
- Cascade to T+10 is the clean-survival band in the extended scenario.
- T+10 to T+25 is the hypoxic-but-survives band in the extended scenario.
- T+5 to T+10 is the hypoxic-but-survives band in the compressed scenario.
- Beyond T+25 extended or beyond T+10 compressed: total DAMCON team loss.

The exact numbers are calibration targets. MAST scripting and playtest can adjust them.

## 4. ACT STRUCTURE

### Act I — Qualification Cruise (~30 minutes)

#### Scene 1: Shipyard Departure and Briefing (~5 min)

Dillon delivers an opening briefing aboard Artemis. The crew has just been assigned to the ship; this is their first cruise together. Dillon explains the qualification framework in TSN terms: each station will be observed across standard operational tasks, and the cruise is expected to be a clean shakedown.

GM does not show qualification cards. Dillon references the framework in general terms but does not enumerate the specific items being tracked.

The captain takes the chair. Helm reports console ready. Each station calls in standard readiness. Comms obtains departure clearance from Kestrel Yards Control. Helm clears the launch envelope and sets course for Tarsis Station.

#### Scene 2: Drill One — Dock and Resupply (~8 min)

Artemis begins with reduced energy and partial weapons complement. The first drill is a dock-and-resupply at Tarsis Station, a friendly TSN facility.

Station functions:

- Helm executes docking approach within tolerance.
- Comms handles station hailing, docking clearance, and resupply request protocols.
- Engineering manages power balance during docking and confirms shields are dropped.
- Captain coordinates the overall sequence.
- Weapons confirms weapons hold during docking proximity.
- Science monitors the station environment.

Dillon observes. If any officer makes a significant procedural error, Dillon may pause the drill and ask for a retry. This is the only point in the mission where Dillon overtly corrects play.

Outcome: Artemis tops up energy reserves and takes on full torpedo complement: eight homing torpedoes, two nukes, two EMPs, two mines. Artemis departs Tarsis.

#### Scene 3: Drill Two — Guided Contact Handling and Controlled Weapons Disable (~9-11 min)

Artemis transits across a nearby quiet operating area. Dillon orders the second drill: a guided, step-by-step contact-handling sequence against Drill Drone 01.

This is the teaching drill. Dillon or Training Control walks the crew through the full ship-contact loop one station beat at a time. The drone does not fight back and does not evade. The task pressure is procedural coordination, not threat.

Drone requirements:

- Initially unidentified to Science at standard sweep.
- Marked as a TSN drill drone after proper scan/classification.
- Holds position.
- Does not evade.
- Does not fire.
- Exposes targetable subsystem: Weapons.
- Survives a normal controlled subsystem-disable pass if the crew does not overfire.

Guided sequence:

1. **Science** scans and classifies the unknown contact.
2. **Comms** hails on standard bands and reports the automated drill-mode response.
3. **Captain** authorizes intercept posture, not fire.
4. **Helm** establishes safe firing geometry: range and arc, not proximity.
5. **Engineering** boosts weapons power to training threshold and reports heat/coolant stable.
6. **Weapons** selects the target, tunes beams from Science data, selects manual subsystem target: Weapons, and holds fire.
7. **Captain** authorizes controlled fire on the Weapons subsystem only.
8. **Weapons** fires until Drone 01's Weapons subsystem is disabled.
9. **Captain** orders ceasefire; **Weapons** confirms weapons safe.
10. **Science** and **Comms** verify disabled state and drone training status.

Exit criteria:

- Science classification complete.
- Comms hail/check complete.
- Captain intercept posture order given.
- Helm firing geometry established.
- Engineering weapons boost/check complete.
- Weapons target/tuning/subsystem-selection check complete.
- Captain controlled-fire authorization given.
- Drone 01 Weapons subsystem disabled.
- Ceasefire confirmed.
- Science/Comms final status verification complete.
- Drone 01 not destroyed before subsystem-disable confirmation.

If Weapons fires before Captain authorization, Dillon pauses the drill and resets to the fire-control step. If Drone 01 is destroyed before Weapons subsystem disable is confirmed, reset Drill Two or mark the relevant station items as PARTIAL/NEEDS RETEST at GM discretion. If Cosmos does not expose a mechanical signal for a check, the GM may manually mark it complete after direct observation.

Station functions:

- Captain separates readiness from authorization, declares subsystem objective, and calls ceasefire.
- Helm establishes geometry on a passive target.
- Weapons demonstrates target selection, tuning, manual Weapons subsystem targeting, controlled fire, and ceasefire.
- Engineering demonstrates weapons boost/overpower and heat/coolant management.
- Science demonstrates classification, frequency/subsystem data, and disable verification.
- Comms demonstrates hail, automated response reporting, and status broadcast relay.

Dillon marks the drill complete only after the Weapons subsystem is disabled and ceasefire is confirmed. The lesson is explicit: identify, hail, position, power, target, authorize, disable, verify.

#### Scene 4: Drill Three — Unguided Evasive Live-Fire Repeat / Engine Disable (~6-8 min)

A second marked target drone is deployed for the transfer drill. Dillon gives the objective and constraints, then stops walking the crew through the sequence.

The intended player experience is: they just learned the contact-handling loop; now they must run it themselves.

Drone requirements:

- Identifiable as a TSN drill drone after scan.
- Uses simple evasion: slow turn, lateral drift, heading changes, or throttle pulses.
- Engagement is live fire against the drone.
- Optional safe/low-risk return fire only if the Cosmos build supports it and playtest confirms it is safe.
- Exposes targetable subsystem: Engines.
- Survives a normal controlled Engine-disable pass if the crew does not overfire.

Dillon states:

- Same contact-handling sequence.
- No step calls this time.
- Drone will evade simply.
- Live fire is authorized after Captain command.
- Exit criterion is Engine subsystem disabled and ceasefire confirmed.

Expected unprompted crew sequence:

1. Science reacquires and classifies Drone 02, including frequency/subsystem data.
2. Comms monitors or hails the training broadcast as appropriate.
3. Captain declares the objective: disable Engines.
4. Helm maintains range and arc against simple evasion.
5. Engineering boosts weapons power and manages heat/coolant.
6. Weapons tunes beams, selects Engines, fires under authorization, and disables Engines.
7. Captain/Weapons cease fire.
8. Science/Comms verify Engine disable.

Exit criteria:

- Drone 02 Engine subsystem disabled.
- Ceasefire confirmed.
- Drone 02 not destroyed before Engine-disable confirmation.

Process quality is observed for qualification, but Drill Three should not silently fail because the crew skipped a nonessential process flag. If they disable Engines without scanning, hailing, or Engineering boost, the drill can complete while the relevant station items receive PARTIAL or NEEDS RETEST.

If the crew stalls completely, Dillon may give one safety-level nudge, not a full walkthrough: "Crew, this is a repeat drill. Apply the previous sequence. Objective remains Engine subsystem disable." Using this nudge is recorded as a development note.

Station functions:

- Captain independently runs the contact-handling sequence and calls ceasefire.
- Helm preserves firing geometry against simple evasion.
- Weapons targets Engines and executes live-fire subsystem disable.
- Engineering supports live fire with power/heat management without being walked through.
- Science provides moving-target data and verifies Engine disable.
- Comms monitors training broadcast and relays status without waiting for prompt.

Dillon marks the drill complete when the Engine subsystem is disabled and ceasefire is confirmed. Artemis is now fully supplied and has demonstrated baseline coordination in both guided and independent contact handling.

### Act II — Pivot and Investigation (~15 minutes)

#### Scene 5: Anderson's New Orders (~3 min)

Command transmits new orders to Artemis. Recorded Anderson clip delivers the briefing.

The orders:

- A fragmentary distress signal has been detected in the Khovan Reach region.
- The signal is on a TSN-compatible emergency band but too degraded for source identification.
- Pattern is consistent with a civilian emergency beacon or a possible TSN vessel in trouble; authenticity cannot be fully resolved from Command's range.
- Artemis is the closest cruiser-class asset with operational capability.
- Orders: investigate the signal source, render aid as appropriate, report findings.
- Expected transit: approximately ninety minutes from current operating area.

Captain acknowledges. Dillon notes that this is now a live operation and the qualification framework remains in effect.

#### Scene 6: Long-Range Transit Toward Khovan Reach (~5 min)

Helm sets course toward the Khovan Reach region. Engineering manages power for sustained high-speed transit. Energy depletion is real and visible; Engineering should report on reserves and sustainable speed.

This is a quiet stretch where the crew settles into operational rhythm. Science conducts long-range passive sweeps. Comms maintains Command reporting cadence. Weapons remains on passive standby. Dillon may comment observationally, but not correctively.

#### Scene 7: Distress Signal Localized (~4 min)

Science resolves the signal enough to locate its source. The readout:

- Source: one vessel, approximately fifteen minutes off Artemis's direct Khovan Reach vector.
- Vessel class: civilian cargo hauler, mid-size.
- Registry: not yet readable due to signal degradation.
- Signal pattern: authentic emergency beacon; no immediate evidence of a tactical lure.
- Life signs: unresolved at long range.

Captain's decision: investigate the signal source now, continue deeper toward Khovan Reach without resolving it, or request further Command guidance.

The intended and doctrinally correct decision is to investigate immediately. This is the signal Anderson ordered Artemis to investigate. A captain who hesitates receives no answer from Dillon. A captain who asks Command may receive delayed acknowledgment, but the operational choice remains the captain's.

If the captain refuses to investigate, use the offramp handling section. The main scenario assumes Artemis deviates to the signal source.

#### Scene 8: Approach to Halcyon Drift (~3 min)

Artemis approaches the signal source.

Science scans and reports:

- Vessel: *Halcyon Drift*, Vesperan-registered civilian cargo hauler.
- Damage: primary reactor offline; hull compromised in two sections; emergency power active.
- Crew: life signs aboard, estimated six to eight individuals.
- Threat: no combat signatures detected.

Comms hails *Halcyon Drift*. Hessler responds, exhausted but coherent. He confirms crew of seven, three injured, reactor regulator failure, emergency power only, and need for professional engineering assistance. He requests an engineering team.

Captain authorizes Engineering deployment. Engineering player leaves the console for the observed GPT-4o Hessler conversation. DAMCON team Reyes, Park, and Achebe deploy with Engineering. MAST removes three DAMCON resources from Artemis until resolved.

### Act III — Halcyon Drift and Khovan Reach (~50 minutes)

#### Scene 9: Away Mission to Halcyon Drift (~10 min)

GPT-4o conversation between Engineering and Hessler, observed by the whole table.

Conversation beats:

1. **Introduction.** Hessler thanks Artemis and escorts Engineering to the damaged section.
2. **Damage walk-through.** Hessler describes the regulator cascade, emergency shutdown, hull stress, and degraded life support.
3. **Diagnostic questioning.** Engineering asks technical questions. Hessler answers from ship logs and crew observations, but he is not a hidden expert.
4. **Failure pattern recognition.** Engineering identifies the regulator defect pattern.
5. **Convergence reveal.** Engineering identifies that the repair requires a quantum field stabilizer rated for civilian reactor systems. Hessler confirms they do not have one. Engineering or the bridge identifies the TSN scientific cache near Khovan Reach as the nearest plausible source.
6. **Repair preparation.** DAMCON begins clearing access to the regulator assembly.
7. **Cascade.** A secondary structural failure triggers atmospheric loss. Blast doors seal. Reyes, Park, and Achebe are trapped in the affected section with limited suit O2.

Engineering reports the full situation to the bridge via Comms: repair requires the cache component; DAMCON is trapped; Engineering can slow atmospheric loss if they stay aboard; captain decision required.

#### Scene 10: The Cascade and the Captain's Decision (~8 min)

The captain now has the full operational picture:

- The DAMCON team is trapped with suit O2 running.
- The repair requires a quantum field stabilizer not aboard *Halcyon Drift* or Artemis.
- The nearest plausible source is the Khovan Reach TSN scientific cache.
- Engineering can mitigate atmospheric loss if they remain aboard *Halcyon Drift*.
- If Engineering returns to Artemis, Artemis has a full bridge crew for the sprint and combat risk, but the DAMCON timer compresses.
- If Artemis does not depart for the cache, the repair cannot succeed.

Captain must decide:

**Engineer stays aboard *Halcyon Drift*.** The DAMCON timer remains extended. Engineering is physically exposed while Artemis runs to the cache.

**Engineer returns to Artemis.** The DAMCON timer compresses. Artemis has full crew complement for the cache run and any pirate encounter.

**Refuse to depart for the cache.** Brick wall. Engineering attempts work for a few minutes and reports that the repair cannot proceed without the stabilizer. Continued refusal produces DAMCON loss and likely *Halcyon Drift* loss.

Captain may also decide whether to convert torpedoes to energy for speed. Each torpedo converted increases operational speed but reduces combat reserves for the pirate encounter and return transit.

This scene establishes the four practical paths.

#### Scene 11: Long-Range Sprint or Run to Khovan Reach (~8-10 min)

Artemis departs for the Khovan Reach cache.

Station functions:

- Helm manages sustained warp and return-vector planning.
- Engineering manages power, heat, and speed sustainability; if still aboard *Halcyon Drift*, this station is partly unavailable on Artemis and operates via comms.
- Weapons reports torpedo reserves and executes conversion if ordered.
- Science scans the cache approach and continues monitoring *Halcyon Drift* where possible.
- Comms relays DAMCON reports, Engineering updates, and Command status.
- Captain maintains the operational picture across the cache run, trapped DAMCON team, and possible external contacts.

DAMCON status reports surface the timer in fiction. Reyes, Park, and Achebe report suit O2, pressure, and seal progress at intervals. The crew should feel urgency without staring at a GM timer.

#### Scene 12: Pirate Arrival Under Salvager Cover (~10 min)

Two unknown vessels arrive at or near *Halcyon Drift*. They identify as independent salvage operators. Working placeholders:

- Vessel One: *Cordial Reach*, claiming Skaraani registry.
- Vessel Two: *Bright Reckoning*, claiming Torgoth registry.

Final species can be changed in Pass 2 to match the preferred Cosmos canonical roster. The important design point is that each claimed identity carries cultural expectations Comms can use.

Their cover story: they picked up the beacon, are investigating salvage rights, and intend to dock with the damaged ship to secure the scene.

The truth: both vessels are pirate craft. They intend to board *Halcyon Drift*, kill witnesses, take cargo, and flee before TSN control is established.

##### Detection route A — hailing and legal/registry probing

Comms can expose the deception before combat by probing any of the following:

- **Registry credentials.** Ask for salvage operator credentials, registry confirmation, current operating-zone permits, and emergency-assistance authority.
- **Legal posture.** State that *Halcyon Drift* is a crewed vessel broadcasting distress and therefore not salvage. Require them to acknowledge rescue law and stand down from any claim.
- **Operational conduct.** Ask why salvage craft are closing to docking distance before TSN scene authority is resolved.
- **Cultural mismatch.** Compare their claimed species/registry style against their behavior. A procedurally precise species evading paperwork is suspicious. A formal honor-coded species dismissing recognized distress protocol is suspicious.
- **Science corroboration.** Ask Science to scan for cargo clamps, boarding gear, weapon signatures, shield geometry, and transponder consistency.

Any one strong tell may justify heightened alert. Two or more justify reporting probable deception to Captain. Science confirmation can convert Comms suspicion into a clear tactical assessment.

##### Detection route B — escalation after denied docking

If Comms does not expose them early, the pirates request docking authorization with *Halcyon Drift*. Captain, Hessler, or Engineering denies docking authorization.

The pirates initially posture as offended salvagers. After a short delay, they attempt to bypass authorization, close to docking range, or lock boarding clamps. Hessler reports unauthorized docking attempt. If Engineering is aboard *Halcyon Drift*, Engineering is now in direct physical danger.

This is the default backstop reveal. It costs time and gives the pirates positional advantage, but it avoids a stalled scene.

##### Combat resolution

Once exposed, the pirates either attack, attempt boarding, or flee depending on Artemis's location and readiness.

Two light-to-moderate pirate vessels are appropriate for a training mission. They should be capable of threatening *Halcyon Drift* and punishing an inattentive Artemis, but not designed to overwhelm a TSN cruiser in competent hands.

Combat station functions:

- Science classifies the pirates and surfaces weapons profiles.
- Weapons engages with subsystem targeting; torpedo reserve decisions matter.
- Helm maneuvers for firing arcs while protecting *Halcyon Drift*.
- Engineering balances combat power and heat.
- Comms broadcasts surrender demands and receives pirate responses.
- Captain authorizes force, sets engagement priorities, and decides whether to accept surrender or pursue fleeing pirates.

The key Captain qualification beat is timing. Authorize force too early, before deception is sufficiently established, and the crew risks firing on apparent civilians. Authorize too late, and the pirates may board or damage *Halcyon Drift*.

#### Scene 13: Cache Retrieval at Khovan Reach (~5 min)

Artemis arrives at the TSN scientific cache. The cache is automated, no personnel aboard.

Comms hails the cache's automated control system, transmits Artemis ID and request codes. Helm docks or station-keeps within transfer envelope.

Science consults cache inventory and identifies the correct component. The inventory should include plausible foils:

- Quantum field stabilizer, civilian-grade — correct.
- Quantum field stabilizer, military-grade — incorrect rating; may damage civilian reactor.
- Quantum field regulator, civilian-grade — wrong component class, similar name.
- Several unrelated items: sensor arrays, hull patches, relay coils, shield emitters.

Science's check is substantive only if Engineering relayed the specifications clearly. If Engineering was vague, Science works from partial information and may choose incorrectly.

If the wrong component is selected, the error is not necessarily discovered at the cache. It may be discovered on return when Engineering attempts installation. That creates a recoverable failure: Artemis must return to the cache for a second attempt, with significant time cost.

#### Scene 14: Return and Rescue Resolution (~10 min)

Artemis returns to *Halcyon Drift* with the component.

Resolution variables:

**DAMCON status.** Outcome depends on timer band:

- Early return: full survival, no medical complications.
- Survival band: hypoxic but alive, medical recovery required.
- Loss threshold exceeded: Reyes, Park, and Achebe are lost.

**Pirate status.** Pirates may have been exposed early, engaged, fled, surrendered, or attempted boarding. If Artemis returns before they fully commit, they may flee. Captain decides whether pursuit is worth the time and risk. If they surrender, Captain must secure prisoners and report captures to Command.

**Halcyon Drift repair.** If the correct component was retrieved, Engineering installs it and stabilizes the reactor. If the wrong component was retrieved and corrected on a second run, repair succeeds late. If the component was never retrieved, *Halcyon Drift* cannot be repaired; surviving crew may be evacuated.

Hessler thanks the crew if the ship is saved. If casualties occurred, the tone remains grave. The mission should acknowledge costs without turning punitive.

#### Scene 15: Return Transit and Debrief (~5-10 min)

Artemis returns to Tarsis or Kestrel Yards depending on damage and remaining energy.

Dillon opens the qualification debrief. Each station receives its qualification dimensions and result. DAMCON casualties, if any, are named. Pirate outcome is recorded. Anderson may provide a brief Command acknowledgment.

End of session.

## 5. THE FOUR DECISION PATHS

The captain's two binary decisions produce four practical paths:

1. Engineering stays aboard *Halcyon Drift* or returns to Artemis.
2. Artemis runs efficiently or converts torpedoes for speed.

### Path A: Engineer stays aboard Halcyon Drift; Artemis runs efficient cache retrieval

DAMCON timer remains extended. Artemis preserves torpedo reserves. Pirates likely arrive while Artemis is away or still returning.

Best outcome: DAMCON team survives, *Halcyon Drift* repaired, pirates exposed and handled without major damage, torpedo reserves preserved.

Costs: Engineering is exposed aboard *Halcyon Drift*. Pirates may attempt docking while Engineering is the physical TSN presence. Comms must manage hailing remotely. Captain has to decide whether to continue the cache run or turn back if the pirate threat escalates.

Failure modes: pirates board or damage *Halcyon Drift* before Artemis returns; Engineering is put at personal risk; remote comms degrade; DAMCON survives only in hypoxic band.

### Path B: Engineer stays aboard Halcyon Drift; Artemis burns torpedoes for speed

DAMCON timer remains extended and Artemis may beat the worst pirate timing. Torpedo reserves are depleted.

Best outcome: Artemis returns with the component before pirates can force the issue; DAMCON team survives cleanly; *Halcyon Drift* repaired; pirates are engaged or driven off with full bridge control restored.

Costs: weapons depletion. If combat happens, Weapons has fewer torpedoes. If something goes wrong on return transit, Artemis is more exposed.

Failure modes: excessive conversion leaves Artemis underarmed for the pirate fight; heat/coolant management becomes strained; captain trades too much combat capacity for speed.

### Path C: Engineer returns to Artemis; Artemis runs efficient cache retrieval

Artemis has full bridge complement for the cache run and pirate encounter, but the DAMCON timer compresses sharply.

Best outcome: if execution is unusually clean and timing generous, DAMCON team reaches hypoxic-but-survives band. More likely, the team dies before Artemis returns.

Costs: people. This is the apparently safe call for the player ship that produces worse rescue outcomes.

Failure modes: DAMCON loss; *Halcyon Drift* deteriorates without mitigation; pirates arrive while no TSN personnel remain physically aboard the casualty ship.

### Path D: Engineer returns to Artemis; Artemis burns torpedoes for speed

DAMCON timer is compressed, but Artemis attempts to beat it through maximum speed.

Best outcome: Artemis returns at the edge of the compressed survival band; DAMCON survives hypoxic; full bridge complement is available for pirate combat; *Halcyon Drift* repaired.

Worst outcome: Artemis arrives too late and is also weapons-depleted.

Costs: high variance. This is a gamble that can work if Helm, Engineering, Weapons, and Captain coordinate tightly.

Failure modes: torpedo depletion plus DAMCON loss; heat/coolant strain; pirate engagement with reduced ordnance.

## 8. MAST SCRIPTING REQUIREMENTS

The scenario requires custom MAST scripting beyond stock Cosmos behavior. This section catalogs requirements.

### State save and reload

A scenario-state save mechanism records progress through the mission. On catastrophic failure, the GM can issue a reload command that restores state to the last drill checkpoint. Qualification items already demonstrated are preserved.

This is the no-fail safety scaffolding.

### DAMCON deployment to Halcyon Drift

A scripted action triggered by captain order or Comms message removes Reyes, Park, and Achebe from Artemis's available DAMCON pool for the duration of the away mission. After resolution, they are restored, marked hypoxic/injured, or marked lost.

Engineering's away-mission representation: the Engineering player physically leaves the console. If SBS requires a mechanical representation, use a state flag for Engineering aboard *Halcyon Drift* versus aboard Artemis.

### Suit O2 timer

Countdown begins at the cascade event.

Configurations:

- Extended: Engineering aboard *Halcyon Drift*, 30-minute countdown.
- Compressed: Engineering aboard Artemis, 15-minute countdown.

Timer surfaces through scripted Comms messages from DAMCON at intervals. Suggested intervals: every 3 minutes extended, every 90 seconds compressed.

Outcome bands:

- Clean survival.
- Hypoxic survival.
- Team loss.

Exact thresholds remain playtest-tunable.

### Pirate arrival timer

Fixed countdown triggers pirate arrival approximately 20 minutes after cascade regardless of Artemis's location. Arrival is announced via Science contact detection and/or Comms hail.

Script variables:

- Pirate cover intact / suspected / exposed.
- Credentials requested yes/no.
- Legal posture challenged yes/no.
- Science scan completed yes/no.
- Docking requested yes/no.
- Docking denied yes/no.
- Unauthorized docking attempt triggered yes/no.
- Combat active yes/no.
- Pirate outcome: fled / surrendered / destroyed / boarded / escaped with cargo.

### Pirate hailing and deception logic

Dialogue should support two reveal modes:

1. **Skillful Comms reveal.** If Comms probes credentials, legal status, cultural mismatch, or operating authority, the pirates respond evasively. GM can mark suspicion or exposure.
2. **Escalation reveal.** If the hailing sequence does not expose them, denial of docking triggers unauthorized approach or attack.

Science scan can accelerate exposure by detecting weapons or boarding equipment inconsistent with civilian salvage operations.

### Cache component identification

A scripted Science check at the Khovan Reach cache. Science officer must identify the correct component from a list. First attempt is the qualification check. Incorrect first attempt is recoverable but costly.

The cache retrieval should not be presented as the mission's original goal. It is a solution discovered through field diagnosis.

### Torpedo-to-energy conversion

Stock Cosmos mechanic. No custom scripting required unless the build wants explicit logging for qualification tracking. Captain calls for conversion; Weapons executes; Engineering manages the resulting energy/heat profile.

### Anderson and Dillon clip playback

Standard Cosmos mission scripting handles audio/video clip playback at trigger points.

Clip triggers:

- Mission start.
- Drill introductions and completions.
- Anderson diversion order.
- Dillon pivot acknowledgment.
- Debrief opening.
- Conditional DAMCON casualty acknowledgment.
- Debrief closing.

### Mission completion conditions

End-state evaluation records:

- DAMCON status: clean survival / hypoxic survival / loss.
- *Halcyon Drift* status: repaired / evacuated / lost.
- Pirate outcome: exposed early / exposed by escalation / fled / surrendered / destroyed / boarded / escaped.
- Artemis status: energy, hull, torpedoes, mines, EMPs, nukes, damage.
- Engineering placement path.
- Torpedo conversion path.
- Cache component first-attempt result.
- Each station's qualification card result.

This data feeds Dillon's debrief.

## 10. DEBRIEF STRUCTURE

Dillon's debrief is where the qualification framework becomes visible.

Flow:

1. Dillon opens with mission-completion statement and operational outcome.
2. Station-by-station review: Captain, Helm, Weapons, Engineering, Science, Comms.
3. Each station hears its qualification dimensions and result: passed, partial, or needs retest.

5. Pirate outcome is reported: deception detected early, exposed by escalation, fled, surrendered, destroyed, or caused damage.

7. Open floor for crew questions.
8. Closing remark.

The debrief is educational, not punitive. Even a needs-retest result is framed developmentally.
